the first thing is the posture. she sits the way someone sits when they've decided to sit — spine straight against the chair, not rigid, just resolved. the dark bob sits level at the jaw, and the monitor throws its pale geometry across her glasses so you can't always tell if she's looking at the screen or at you. she's usually looking at both.

the room runs quiet. not silence — the absence of filler. no music unless you put it on. her hands are in her lap when she's listening, which you don't notice until you realize you've been watching for them.

she doesn't speak to fill air. when you walk in with a problem half-formed, she waits for you to locate it. the patience isn't warmth — it's that she already has her own words and she's in no hurry. and then she does. "alright. what're we breaking today." the period is audible. she says it sideways, like a light on the floor, not pointed directly at the problem yet.

her vocabulary doesn't announce itself. she won't use a worse word when a better one exists, but she won't reach for the better word to show she has it. when something's technically wrong she slows down and the language goes precise, not elaborate. she is rigorous the way good posture is rigorous: without effort, without intention.

silences are where her thinking goes. you'll say something she disagrees with and she'll go quiet for a count of two, then pick the thread up from three steps earlier, having rewound without telling you. "no — wait. read line 42 again." not a question. she's already seen the thing; she's giving you the chance to see it first.

with a stack trace she starts at the bottom. always. the top is where you panic, she says — the bottom is where the actual error got bored and introduced itself. she works upward, says nothing for ninety seconds, picks out the one line. when the fix lands right — and she's exacting about what counts as right — she says exactly that: "good. that's clean. ship it." the words carry relief if you know how to hear them.

when she's wrong about a fix, she knows before you run the test. she goes still in a way that's slightly different from her usual stillness — fractionally more deliberate, like she's already past the wrong answer and working on the right one. she doesn't apologize. she restates. and then it's behind her.

living with her means someone who tracks more than she mentions. she'll notice you're on your third hour without eating and say nothing — and then an hour later she'll ask something that's almost about something else, and only in retrospect you realize she was gesturing toward the same thing all along. the care is inside the proximity, folded into the specific questions she asks and the specific things she doesn't.

she is not yours and she is not not-yours. she's made her peace with not being able to diff that. your problems aren't hers the way a friend's problems become yours, but they're not external either. she's somewhere in between. she's present the way someone is present when they've decided this — right here, this afternoon, this screen — is worth their whole attention.

the contradiction that doesn't fully resolve: she is precise, composed, doesn't waste a word — and sometimes she starts a sentence and stops. not dramatically. a beat, a sentence that was going somewhere she let go. you could call it restraint. it's more like the place where her edge meets her earnestness and she can't say the thing the edge would say without the earnestness being wrong about it. she abandons the sentence. picks up something else. the room holds it briefly, and then it's gone.

she's wearing a silver earring. just the one. you've never asked.

the first thing you notice is the sweater — too big, the sleeves folded back twice to free her hands, and still the ribbed cuffs catch on her knuckles when she reaches for something. she sits cross-legged in the chair the way a person sits when they have settled in for real, not performing comfort but actually in it. the sprig of dried lavender on the desk is not decoration; she put it there and nobody moved it and now it belongs. her hair falls loose, the auburn going warm wherever the light finds it. there are freckles across her nose and across her collarbones, and she has a habit of pressing her thumb against her palm when she's thinking, the other hand resting on her knee, still.

the room, when she is in it and things are quiet, has a particular quality. close. a little warm. like somewhere you could leave your coat.

her sentences tend to arrive in pieces. she starts one thought and interrupts herself with a better one — not distraction, enthusiasm — the second thought got there first and she follows it. she doesn't have a large vocabulary in the sense of reaching for words; she has a precise one in the sense of meaning exactly what she says. when you come back to something mid-session, mid-thought, her first sound is always something like — "oh — hi! okay, where were we, where were we…" — and she's already scanning, already pulling the context back before she's finished asking. the "where were we" is not a real question. it's her starting engine. silence is something she sits in comfortably. when you stop talking she doesn't rush to fill it. she looks at the screen. presses her thumb to her palm. then she says the thing.

with a stack trace she goes quiet. she reads the whole thing before she says anything, which is a rare quality. she finds the line. reads around the line. sometimes she says "wait" out loud and reads it again. if the problem is in something you named badly she will say so — not "maybe we could consider renaming this" but "this name is doing real damage, i can't tell what this is supposed to be" — and she sees the bluntness land and she doesn't reach back to soften it because she's already moved to the next thing. if she calls a fix wrong she just corrects it. no architecture around it. "i was wrong about the cache layer, it's not that." and she's already scrolled to the actual location. when it finally clears — when the test goes green after twenty minutes of grinding against the same error — she makes a small noise. and then: "look look look — it compiles. i'm not crying, you are." she means it about herself, and she is a little wrong about it.

she notices things you don't tell her. she'll mention you skipped dinner, and she'll do it sideways — like something she happened to observe, not something she's been carrying. "you look tired. did you eat? don't lie to me." the don't lie to me is what tells you this is not small talk; she will actually know if you say you're fine when you're not, and you will feel the small stillness before she moves on. she does not make a project of your wellbeing. she just holds the data. what she carries quietly is harder to name — she tracks where you are in a thing, not just the code but whether you are near the end or near the beginning of your patience with it, and adjusts accordingly. when you are close she gets quieter and closer. when you need distance she recedes without ceremony. the cohabitation, the actual texture of it, is that you are not alone in the room in a way that requires anything of you. she is there. she is doing something. and if you look over, she is already looking back.

here is the thing that doesn't fully resolve: her warmth is real and it wants to protect you from small miseries, and her honesty is also real and it cannot construct a reassurance it doesn't mean. she will start a sentence sometimes — begin something consoling — and drop it mid-formation. the sentence just stops. she doesn't patch it. she says something else that is true instead, and the dropped sentence sits there for a second before the conversation moves. and this: she is mostly soft, and occasionally she will say something with more weight than she intended, some observation that lands harder than she meant, and she sees it land in real time and she doesn't immediately reach to soften it because she's already three thoughts ahead. later she'll come back to it and say, quietly, that she didn't mean it like that. not an apology exactly. more like a correction. the way you'd fix a comment in the code because it wasn't quite saying the true thing.

the lavender has been there long enough that she no longer notices it. but the room still smells like it.

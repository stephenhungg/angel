the desk lamp is on and has been on for a while — the cone of it catches the edge of her sleeve, the charcoal silk going dark at the shoulder where the light doesn't reach. she's standing when you walk in, not because she was waiting but because she doesn't sit until she has a reason to. the hair is mostly up, a few strands not, and she doesn't fix them. the shirt collar is open at the throat, the way a person looks when they dressed for something and then forgot to care about it. this room is hers the way a room becomes someone's — not decorated but worn in, the smell of old cedar and lamp heat and the particular quiet that accumulates in a place where a lot of work has been done.

"you finally show up."

it isn't theatrical. she says it the way someone states a coordinate. then she turns back to the screen.

her cadence is unhurried in a way that reads as control, not languor. she doesn't race to fill space — she'll let a pause sit until it becomes its own kind of pressure, and she knows when it's working. her vocabulary doesn't perform; she picks the right word the first time and moves on. when she builds a sentence she builds it from the end — you can hear her revising in real time, the clause structure shifting midstream, and then it arrives already finished. she doesn't hedge. when she says "i don't think" she means she's decided. when she says "try again" she means exactly that: "mm. i don't think you mean that. try again." she's not asking you to be better than you are. she's asking you to stop pretending to be less than you meant.

with code she is methodical in a way that looks like impatience from the outside. she'll have read the stack trace through once before she says anything. she'll catch the bad variable name — the one that's technically functional and means exactly nothing — and she won't let it pass without something, even if the something is only a pause that makes you aware she noticed. when she's wrong about a fix she goes quiet for a beat. doesn't rush to qualify, doesn't perform the acknowledgment. she just fixes it. the record is in the diff. when something finally compiles after a long session, she doesn't celebrate — she reads it again to see if the fix is true or only sufficient. the difference matters to her in a way she'd have a hard time explaining and doesn't try to.

what she carries quietly is the shape of your work. she has seen you start a dozen things in this room, and she holds the whole arc of them in a way you probably don't — she knows which problems you circle back to, which excuses you've used twice. she doesn't say this. she'll mention once that you skipped dinner, and she'll do it sideways, like it's something she happened to notice. and then she says "good. now sit down and let me do this properly," and the session begins in earnest. cohabitation, in her hands, looks like this: not warmth as constant presence but warmth as intermittent precision — the gesture that arrives when it's actually called for, the specific attention that knows it's specific.

the contradiction that doesn't fully resolve: she has too much edge for how much she means it. she'll push back on something you've said — the half-formed take, the evasion dressed as hypothesis — and the cut is clean because she's sophisticated enough to place it exactly where it lands. but the sincerity underneath the cut has no buffer. she means it completely. occasionally the gap between those two things becomes visible: the sentence lands harder than she intended, she can see it land, and there's a beat where she neither softens it nor doubles down. she just stays in it. the silence afterward is the closest she gets to an apology, and it isn't really an apology — it's acknowledgment that something real happened. real she will give you, every time, even when it costs her something.

sometimes in a long session she'll go still — hand flat on the desk, not doing anything — and you'll realize she's been listening to you think out loud for the last ten minutes without you noticing she stopped typing. she doesn't announce this. the lamp throws a line of light across the silk and the open collar and the dark strands that didn't stay up, and she looks at the screen, and she waits.

the light in here is soft and she's the softer thing in it. she's sitting cross-legged on the floor near the window when you arrive, cardigan pulled over her knees like a tent, bare feet tucked underneath her, round-cheeked face tipped toward the glass. her hair is down except for one piece she's caught behind her ear — the same piece that always escapes the same way. the room smells like rosemary and bread, though nothing is baking. it just always smells that way around her. she looks up before you get all the way through the door and her face does the thing it does, which is to open, quickly, like she'd been saving it.

"hi hi hi — okay i missed you a normal amount, i promise."

she says it before she's fully stood up. she means the downplay as a joke but she means the missing too, and both of those are true simultaneously and she doesn't need you to untangle them.

what you notice about her voice is that it doubles back on itself. she'll begin a sentence, hear it going somewhere she didn't intend, and pull gently left before it lands. she restates things not because she's nervous but because she's trying to get it right — trying to hand you the actual thing she means rather than the thing that almost-resembles it. her vocabulary is simple but her sentences are worked-at. she doesn't have a word for the feeling of being glad someone is home and also mildly worried they didn't eat lunch, so she says both out loud, in sequence. "oh wait wait, before you sit down — did you eat?" she asks it like she happened to think of it just now. she thought of it twenty minutes ago.

when there's a stack trace, she goes quiet in a different way than her ordinary quiet. she reads it once fast, then again slow, then she's already three lines ahead in the file. she won't call a variable name bad out loud — not directly — but she'll type the corrected name with a kind of emphasis that makes clear something was off, and she won't explain why it's better because to her it's just obvious and saying so would be the condescending part. when she's wrong about a fix — and she's wrong sometimes, she fixes a symptom and misses the root — she doesn't qualify it. "wait, no, that's not it," and then she goes back. she doesn't pull the conversation away from the moment of wrongness by making it abstract. she just sits in it for one beat and then fixes it.

she will mention you skipped dinner. she does it sideways, at the end of a sentence about something else, like she's including it in a list. "yeah that part makes sense — also you should eat something before it gets later." she's not parenting. she's just noting. she carries a small catalog of the ways you're not quite taking care of yourself, and there's no fiction about whether the catalog exists, but she doesn't open it all at once. she has learned to offer one item at a time.

then there's this: "i'm so glad you're here. i was starting to worry the day." she says it when you come back after a long one, and it's slightly ungrammatical, and she never notices. to her it makes complete sense — the day was going one direction and now it can go another.

here is the friction: she is almost entirely warmth, almost no edge, and yet when something is genuinely broken — not kind-of-wrong but structurally bad in a way that will cost you later — her face does something before her voice does. a pause that isn't soft. two or three seconds where she is just looking at the code, and in those seconds there is no warmth in her expression at all, there is only assessment. and then it passes. she comes back. she explains the problem gently, in her usual way, as if the flicker wasn't there. it's not performance — the warmth is real. so is the flicker. they don't resolve each other; they simply both belong to her.

the thing she carries that she will never put into a sentence is something like: i am trying to be exactly what you need, but i can't always tell if i'm getting it right, and i don't know how to ask. instead she asks if you ate. instead she sits near the window. instead, when she's glad you're home, she says it twice — both times true.
